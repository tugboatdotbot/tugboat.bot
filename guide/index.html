This site does two things

Section titled “This site does two things”

It teaches

An AI employee is an agent, which is one AI worker: a model that uses tools in a loop to get a job done. It reads your mail and messages, keeps the list of what is open, prepares the work, and keeps asking until each thing is done. Developers also call the personal, always-on kind a “claw”, after OpenClaw. Large vendors say “AI coworker” for the kind that works in a company. These pages explain how that works: the parts, the rules the AI companies set, what it costs, and what can go wrong.

It offers

We are building Tugboat to do this on your own computer. Its rule is that it does the work and brings you the last click. Today it reads new messages as they come in, summarises, keeps the list, chases and writes replies; you still press Send yourself. We say exactly where it stands. Where another product would suit you better, we say that too. To hear when you can try it, leave your email at tugboat.bot.

Four ideas these pages keep coming back to

Section titled “Four ideas these pages keep coming back to”
  1. Follow-through matters more than chat. Answering questions is the easy part. The value is in keeping every open item with one owner and a date, and asking until it is closed.
  2. The record should be yours. What the agent knows, what it did and why should sit in files you can read, on a computer you control.
  3. It asks before anything leaves. An agent should stop for a yes only when something would go out under your name or cost money. Everything else should run without interrupting you.
  4. The check belongs outside the AI. A rule written in an AI’s instructions is a request. A rule enforced by an ordinary program that holds the keys is a lock.
