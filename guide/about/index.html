About this site

For: everyone. Last updated: November 10, 2026.

The short version

Section titled “The short version”
  • This site explains how AI employees and personal AI assistants work, and describes Tugboat, the assistant we are building.
  • We run one for ourselves. Since November 11 it is one AI employee on a Windows PC. An older team of agents, several AI workers that split one person’s work by topic, still runs beside it. Much of what is here comes from building it and watching it break.
  • Pages are drafted with AI models, checked by a second model against the sources, and read by a person before they are published.
  • We make one of the products we compare. We try to be fair, and you should still read us knowing that.

Why the site exists

Section titled “Why the site exists”

An owner deciding whether to try an AI employee finds it hard to get a straight account of what it does, what it costs and what can go wrong. A builder finds it hard to learn what the AI companies actually allow.

We needed those answers ourselves. These pages are the answers we found, written so that someone outside the project can use them.

How a page is made

Section titled “How a page is made”
  1. It starts from working notes. While building Tugboat, we keep a record of each decision: the options, the reasons, and what would change our mind. The public pages are written from that record.
  2. An AI model drafts the page from a written brief: the reader, the question, the sources.
  3. A second AI model checks the draft against the same sources. It does not see the first model’s reasoning. It lists claims that the sources do not support.
  4. A person reads the final wording and decides whether it is published.

We say this openly because a site about AI should be plain about its own use of it.

What we check, and what we do not

Section titled “What we check, and what we do not”
  • Rules and prices are read from the companies’ own pages where possible, and dated. They change often. Follow the source link before you rely on one.
  • Other products are described from their documentation, their code where it is open, news coverage and review sites. Unless a page says we ran something, we did not run it.
  • Tugboat is described as it is today. Where Tugboat stands lists what does not work yet.
  • Nothing here is legal advice. Where we explain a company’s terms, it is our reading of public pages on the date shown.

Where we are biased

Section titled “Where we are biased”

We believe a few things, and they shape what we build and how we compare:

  • Your records should be on your own computer, in files you can read.
  • An agent should prepare the work, and a person should say yes before anything goes out in their name.
  • Keeping every item until it is closed matters more than a clever conversation.

If you weigh those differently, a hosted product may be the right choice for you. Our comparison says when.

Corrections

Section titled “Corrections”

If something here is wrong or out of date, we want to fix it. Write to hello@tugboat.bot. A page that has been corrected says so, with the date.
