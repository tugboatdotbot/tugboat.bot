What is a day with an AI employee like?

For: the owner, the decider in a team. Last updated: November 13, 2026.

The short version

Section titled “The short version”
  • The idea in one line: it does the work and brings you the last click.
  • In the morning it tells you what arrived that matters and what is still open.
  • During the day it stays quiet, unless a deadline is close, a person is waiting on you, or something you asked it to watch for has happened.
  • When something needs doing, it prepares it first: the reply is written and the file attached. You tap Send, Edit or No.
  • Nothing leaves in your name without your tap on the exact text.
  • Most of this has run for one person since November 11 to 13, 2026. Sending itself does not yet: you press Send in Gmail. The table at the end says what runs.

The names, numbers and messages on this page are invented.

Why it is built this way

Section titled “Why it is built this way”

Most things that slip do not slip because nobody knew about them. They slip at the last small step: the reply that was never written, the document that was never looked up, the form that was never sent.

A list that only reminds you does not fix that. So Tugboat’s agent, which is one AI worker that uses tools in a loop to get a job done, is given a rule:

  • If it is fairly sure what to do, it does everything up to the final step, shows you exactly what will happen, and asks for one tap.
  • If it is not sure, it asks one short question first.
  • It never just reports that something is waiting.

8:00. One message

Section titled “8:00. One message”

Each morning one message arrives on the page. This part has run since November 11, 2026. It looks like this:

  • At the top: a warning when one of your apps has not been copied recently. The message never reports “nothing new” when it simply could not see.
  • Then how much arrived, per app.
  • Then at most seven things that matter, each with one line on what it is and one on why.
  • Then two short lists: who is waiting on you, and who you are waiting on, with how long.

A tap on any line opens the message it came from. Right and Wrong mark each judgment. Those marks are how you find out whether it picks the right things.

“214 arrived, 6 matter” is the point. The other 208 are newsletters, receipts and notifications. They are counted, not listed.

Since November 12 the same message also lists what is open, and which of those wait on you.

A card

Section titled “A card”

When something needs you, it arrives as a card. This is the heart of it. Reply cards for email have run since November 12. This picture shows the full design:

  • Send: it goes out exactly as shown, once. (Today: it opens in Gmail, filled in, for you to send.)
  • Edit: you change the text. Your version becomes the one that can be sent.
  • Not now: it comes back at the next round-up, the summary it sends each morning and evening, or sooner if the deadline is close.
  • No: it is dropped, and you can say why in one tap. Those answers are how its judgment is measured.

The card is drawn by the gateway, an ordinary program with no AI in it, from what the agent proposed. It shows which address the message goes from, every recipient in full, and the whole text. The agent cannot change a proposal after you have seen it, and it has no tool that sends.

During the day

Section titled “During the day”

It stays quiet. It interrupts for three reasons only, and it says which one:

  1. A deadline is close.
  2. A person is waiting on you, and has waited longer than is reasonable. (Designed; not yet on.)
  3. Something you asked it to watch for has happened: “Your parcel has arrived.”

Everything else waits for the next round-up. An assistant that is supposed to reduce the noise in your day must not add to it. It never interrupts at night, and at most five times a day. A thing you asked it to watch for is checked every half hour until it happens.

When you talk to it

Section titled “When you talk to it”

You write to it on a web page, or dictate. You can ask it what you would ask a good assistant:

  • “Who is waiting on me?”
  • “What did the landlord say about the deposit?”
  • “Remind me Friday about the insurance.”
  • “Draft a reply to that.”

A simple answer takes a few seconds. One that looks through your messages has taken between 4 and 20 seconds. It looks things up in your own mail and messages, which it may read and may not change.

18:00. The evening list

Section titled “18:00. The evening list”

Closed today: 4. Still open: 3. The insurance form waits on you (due Friday). The quote waits on the plumber, asked two days ago. The résumé went to Dana at 10:12; no answer yet.

This list is made by the gateway itself, from its own record, with no AI involved. So it arrives even on a day when the AI is unavailable.

If the computer was off

Section titled “If the computer was off”

When it is running again, it catches up once and tells you what it missed. It does not send one message per missed morning. This is built and has passed its tests; it has not yet been seen after a real power-off.
