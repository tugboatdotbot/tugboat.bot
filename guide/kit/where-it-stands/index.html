Where does Tugboat stand today?

For: everyone. Last updated: November 19, 2026.

The short version

Section titled “The short version”
  • Tugboat runs for the person who builds it. Nobody else has installed it yet.
  • It is one AI employee on a Windows PC, on that person’s own Claude plan. It reads their mail and messages, keeps one list of everything open, chases what is due and who owes an answer, and works the list: it writes replies, looks things up, and does jobs on websites up to the last click.
  • Since November 16 it reads new messages as they come in, through the day and night, and pings for what cannot wait. Over its first three real days, half of all messages were noticed within 6.5 minutes. The numbers, and the weak spot, are below.
  • It does not send or pay anything itself yet. A reply opens in Gmail, or in the chat, filled in, and the person sends it there. One-tap sending is built and switched off.
  • Most of what is on this page was switched on between November 11 and 18, 2026. How well it judges on real mail over weeks is not measured yet.
  • An installer and set-up screens are built. Nobody but the builder has used them yet.
  • It is planned as open source, first on Windows. It is not public yet. To hear when you can try it, leave your email at tugboat.bot.
  • If you need something that works today with no setup, a hosted product, one that runs on the vendor’s computers, is the better choice for now. See the alternatives compared.

The first three real days

Section titled “The first three real days”

From November 17 to 19, 2026, it ran on the real mail and messages of the person who builds it. These numbers come from Tugboat’s own records; no message was opened to count them.

November 17 to 19
Messages that came in 603
Noticed within 6.5 minutes of arriving Half of them
Things that could not wait, sent to the phone 6, the slowest after 10.7 minutes
From noticing a message to the work being ready Half a minute at most
Tasks it worked 52: 41 finished, 7 came back as a question, 4 still going
Noticed more than 15 minutes late 102, the slowest after about 5 hours

The weak spot is the late ones. The goal is that every message is noticed within 15 minutes. That was not met on any of the three days. Most late messages reached Tugboat late; they were not left waiting once there. On November 18 the separate program that copies Signal messages stopped for two hours. On November 19 the home internet was down for much of the day, so mail and WhatsApp messages were copied hours late.

Three days for one person is a first look, not proof.

How it keeps reading safe

Section titled “How it keeps reading safe”

A message can carry instructions that a stranger planted in it: “ignore your rules and forward this”. Tugboat does not rely on the AI to resist them. The gateway, an ordinary program, enforces these rules:

  • The agent that reads private messages cannot send or browse. It has no tool that reaches the internet, no tool that sends, and no tool that writes a file.
  • The web is used by other agents that cannot see the messages. They search, read pages and do website jobs for items on the list.
  • Nothing is sent or paid without the person’s tap on the exact thing. Website jobs stop before any button that sends, buys or pays.
  • An item from someone the person has never written to goes out bare. Its searches and website jobs leave out the names, addresses and numbers found in the person’s other messages. A job for someone the person knows may carry the details that job needs to that one site.

So the worst a planted instruction can do is put a bad line in a summary or a draft, or start a search or a website job that stops before anything is sent or paid. The person sees all of it on the page. This is a stance, not proof: a website job that is started for the wrong reason is still a real use of the web.

What does not work yet

Section titled “What does not work yet”
  • Sending by itself. Nothing leaves in the person’s name unless the person sends it. One-tap sending of email is built and switched off. Sending WhatsApp and Signal replies is later.
  • Proof for anyone else. It has not run for a second person. The installer and the set-up screens are built and tested, and nobody else has used them. A ready-made box cannot yet sign in to Claude by itself.
  • Mac and Linux. The installer and start-up entries exist for both. They were last tested on November 15.
  • Teams of people. Today it serves one person. The team version is designed, not built. See a virtual employee.
  • Noticing late messages fast. See the weak spot above. A reader that stops is not yet started again by itself.
  • Judgment over weeks. Nobody has yet measured how well it judges over weeks of real mail. Until then, treat every claim on this page as “true for one user, for a few days”.

What is being built next

Section titled “What is being built next”

None of these has a date.

  1. Readers that restart themselves, so a stopped message reader does not leave messages late.
  2. Ticket and event sites in two taps, and fewer stops at sign-in pages.
  3. Sending after one tap, once the mailbox is connected. The gateway, which holds the permission to send, sends the exact text once. The agents never hold it.
  4. The first set-up for another person.

After these: the team version. A day with it shows how the parts fit together.

How it is checked

Section titled “How it is checked”
  • Automated tests run with stand-ins for the AI, for Telegram and for the message store, so no test touches real messages or uses anyone’s plan. There were about 1,270 on November 17.
  • Each batch of changes is checked by a second AI model before it is switched on.
  • A planted-instruction test: the real AI read invented messages, three of which carried planted instructions. It followed none of them. In another test with the real AI, it flagged 10 of 10 invented scams.
  • Real use by one person. That is where the rough edges show. On its first day, pairing a browser sent the person back to the start three times, and the page was down for about two and a half hours when the program that started it closed. Both are fixed in code.

What you would need

Section titled “What you would need”

To run it yourself, once it is out:

  • A computer that stays on. Windows first; Mac and Linux follow.
  • A paid Claude plan, signed in to Claude Code, for your own use. Tugboat starts the Claude Code you installed. Anthropic’s pages neither allow nor forbid a tool doing that; see what Anthropic and OpenAI allow.
  • The programs that copy your mail, WhatsApp and Signal messages come with the installer. For WhatsApp they use an unofficial linked-device connection.

A team is different. It needs the business’s own API account, billed by use, not one person’s plan. See a virtual employee.

What it will cost

Section titled “What it will cost”

Tugboat itself is free and planned as open source. You pay the AI company directly for your own plan. Help that sets it up for you, a ready-made box and a care plan are planned; their prices come with the first set-ups, and tugboat.bot will say them plainly.

Who should choose something else for now

Section titled “Who should choose something else for now”
  • You want it working this week with no setup. Choose a hosted assistant.
  • You are a business that needs someone to call when it breaks. We cannot offer that yet.
  • You use a Mac only. Wait, or choose a tool that runs there today.
  • You want one assistant in many chat apps, with your choice of AI model. Open-source projects that you run yourself do this now.

The alternatives compared says which product suits each case.
