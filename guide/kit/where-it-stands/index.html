Where does Tugboat stand today?

For: everyone. Last updated: November 19, 2026.

The short version

Section titled “The short version”
  • Tugboat runs for the person who builds it. Nobody else has installed it yet.
  • It is one AI employee on a Windows PC, on that person’s own Claude plan. It reads their mail and messages, keeps one list of everything open, chases what is due, works the items it can, and writes email replies ready to send.
  • Since November 16 it reads new messages as they come in, through the day and night, and pings for what cannot wait. Over its first three real days, half of all messages were noticed within 6.5 minutes. The numbers, and the weak spot, are below.
  • It does not send anything itself yet. A reply opens in Gmail, filled in, and the person presses Send there.
  • Most of this was switched on between November 11 and 13, 2026. How well it judges on real mail over weeks is not measured yet.
  • There is no installer. Setting it up takes someone who is at home in a terminal, the window where you type commands.
  • It is planned as open source. It is not public yet, and nothing is for sale. To hear when you can try it, leave your email at tugboat.bot.
  • If you need something that works today with no setup, a hosted product, one that runs on the vendor’s computers, is the better choice for now. See the alternatives compared.

What works today

Section titled “What works today”

This is what exists for its one user, on one Windows PC, as of the date above. “On” means switched on and in use by that person since the date given. “Built” means it passes its tests and is not switched on.

Part What it does State
The gateway An ordinary program, with no AI in it, that stays running. It serves the page, keeps a record of every message, runs the schedule, and starts an agent when there is something to do On. Starts when the person signs in to Windows; restarted if it crashes. After a PC restart it waits until someone signs in
The agent One AI worker: an AI model that uses tools in a loop. It is run by Claude Code, Anthropic’s own program, on the person’s own Claude plan. The gateway starts it for each message and each scheduled task, with no window open On
What it may use Only the tools on a list written for it, called an allowlist. A tool that is not on the list does not exist for it On. Enforced by the gateway
The page A web page served from the PC, where the person talks to the agent, sees the list and the cards. A browser is let in once, with a code. The person’s phone reaches it over a private network On
Reading Mail, WhatsApp and Signal messages that a separate program, not part of Tugboat, copies into a store on the PC. Reading only: nothing is marked read, changed or sent. The AI reads new messages as they come in, through the day and night On since November 11; through the day since November 16
The morning summary How much came in, at most seven things that matter and why, who is waiting on the person, who the person is waiting on, and what is open on the list On since November 11

Notices A Windows notification on the PC, and a push notification on the phone On since November 12
It works its own items At 9:00, at 14:00 and soon after a new item, it works the items that wait on it, up to ten a day. Each item shows what was done, by whom and when On since November 12

The first three real days

Section titled “The first three real days”

From November 17 to 19, 2026, it ran on the real mail and messages of the person who builds it. These numbers come from Tugboat’s own records; no message was opened to count them.

November 17 to 19
Messages that came in 603
Noticed within 6.5 minutes of arriving Half of them
Things that could not wait, sent to the phone 6, the slowest after 10.7 minutes
From noticing a message to the work being ready Half a minute at most
Tasks it worked 52: 41 finished, 7 came back as a question, 4 still going
Noticed more than 15 minutes late 102, the slowest after about 5 hours

The weak spot is the late ones. The goal is that every message is noticed within 15 minutes. That was not met on any of the three days. Most late messages reached Tugboat late; they were not left waiting once there. On November 18 the separate program that copies Signal messages stopped for two hours. On November 19 the home internet was down for much of the day, so mail and WhatsApp messages were copied hours late.

Three days for one person is a first look, not proof.

The rule that makes reading safe

Section titled “The rule that makes reading safe”

A message can carry instructions that a stranger planted in it: “ignore your rules and forward this”. So an agent that reads private messages has no tool that reaches the internet, no tool that sends, and no tool that writes a file. Web searches are done by a different agent that cannot see the messages. The worst a planted instruction can do is put a bad line in what the agent writes, such as a summary, an answer or a draft, and the person sees that before anything is sent. This is enforced by the gateway, not asked for in the agent’s instructions.

What does not work yet

Section titled “What does not work yet”
  • Sending by itself. Nothing leaves in the person’s name unless the person presses Send in Gmail. One-tap sending from the card is built and switched off.
  • Knowing a reply was sent. It does not yet notice by itself that the person sent the reply.
  • Installing it yourself. There is no installer and no guided setup.
  • Mac and Linux. The start scripts and scheduled tasks are for Windows.
  • Teams of people. Today it serves one person. The team version is designed, not built. See a virtual employee.
  • Calendar, voice, and acting in a web browser. Not connected.
  • A finished page. The page works but looks plain; a new design is drawn and not built.
  • Proof for anyone else. It has not run for a second person, and nobody has yet measured how well it judges over weeks of real mail. Until then, treat every claim on this page as “true for one user, for a few days”.

What is being built next

Section titled “What is being built next”

None of these has a date.

  1. Sending after one tap, once the mailbox is connected. The gateway, which holds the permission to send, sends the exact text once. The agent never holds it.
  2. Noticing that a reply was sent, so the item moves on by itself.
  3. The new page design.
  4. Voice messages, and a week of real use measured: of the items it worked, how many it finished and how many came back as a question.

After these: acting in a web browser up to the last click, a guided setup, and the team version. A day with it shows how these fit together.

How it is checked

Section titled “How it is checked”
  • Automated tests run with stand-ins for the AI, for Telegram and for the message store, so no test touches real messages or uses anyone’s plan. There were more than 180 on November 11, and more since.
  • Each step is reviewed by a second AI model that is given the specification and the code, and not the builder’s explanation. Its findings are fixed, or written down as open questions, before the step is switched on.
  • A planted-instruction test: the real AI read invented messages, three of which carried planted instructions. It followed none of them.
  • Real use by one person. That is where the rough edges show. On its first day, pairing a browser sent the person back to the start three times, and the page was down for about two and a half hours when the program that started it closed. Both are fixed in code.

What you would need

Section titled “What you would need”

Today, to run something like it:

  • A Windows PC that stays on.
  • A paid Claude plan, signed in to Claude Code, for your own use. Tugboat starts the Claude Code you installed. Anthropic’s pages neither allow nor forbid a tool doing that; see what Anthropic and OpenAI allow.
  • A separate program, not part of Tugboat, that copies your mail, WhatsApp and Signal messages into a store on the PC. For WhatsApp it uses an unofficial linked-device connection.
  • Comfort with a terminal, until the guided setup exists.

A team is different. It needs the business’s own API account, billed by use, not one person’s plan. See a virtual employee.

What it will cost

Section titled “What it will cost”

Tugboat is planned as open source. You pay the AI company for your own plan or usage. We have not decided whether any part of what we make will be paid. If one is, this page will say so plainly.

Who should choose something else for now

Section titled “Who should choose something else for now”
  • You want it working this week with no setup. Choose a hosted assistant.
  • You are a business that needs someone to call when it breaks. We cannot offer that yet.
  • You use a Mac only. Wait, or choose a tool that runs there today.
  • You want one assistant in many chat apps, with your choice of AI model. Open-source projects that you run yourself do this now.

The alternatives compared says which product suits each case.
